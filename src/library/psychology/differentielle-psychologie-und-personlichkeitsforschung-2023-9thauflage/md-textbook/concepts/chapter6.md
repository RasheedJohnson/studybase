## Core concept clusters

### 6.1
Creativity boom after Guilford (1950) and Sputnik; definitions centering on novelty plus usefulness (Barron, Stein, MacKinnon); scientific progress driven more by tests than verbal definitions.

### 6.2 / 6.2.1-6.2.4
Idiographic vs nomothetic tension; historiometric analysis; 4 P-U model; measurement methods (biographical, self/other ratings, psychometric); open-ended scoring and reliability limits; Guilford divergent-thinking facets (not equating creativity with divergent tests alone); Torrance Alternate Uses; Remote Associates Test; association gradients; questionnaires and Openness links.

### 6.3.1-6.3.4
Criterion multidimensionality and peak age; expert-criterion validities; creativity-IQ relation and contested threshold model; incremental school prediction; personality profile of high creatives; instruction and partialling effects.

### 6.4
Product-first selection; biographical inventories; family stimulation; cross-sectional limits on prediction claims.

### 6.5.1-6.5.2
Wallas stages; Weisberg expertise critique; attention (defocused / switching); flat associations; low arousal; latent inhibition; investment theory; Kirton adaptor-innovator; Amabile climate; Eysenck psychoticism model and later Openness findings.

### 6.6
Construct status vs intelligence; stability and prediction deficits; parenting correlates; trainability skepticism ("fostered, not forced").

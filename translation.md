# Part I: Foundations and Research Methods

## 1 Foundations

### 1.1 Introduction

As every everyday experience teaches, the distinguishability of individuals is one of the fundamental facts of life itself. Individual particularities are already evident immediately after birth and intensify with increasing maturation. No one is equal to another in appearance, gesture, facial expression and demeanor, in ways of thinking, opinions and attitudes, in language and behavior. Everyone reacts in their own particular way to their environment and to other people.

Even when certain correspondences exist between several persons, these apply at most to narrowly circumscribed characteristics. The particularity of the person, by contrast, becomes recognizable in the specific combination of traits. Even when one considers only a single trait, according to an old proverb, the same thing done by different people need not be the same. This points to the specific motives for behavior or to the not exactly repeatable situational embedding of behavior, and underscores the unity and uniqueness of the person.

Since time immemorial, individual particularities have been observed and recorded. They provide the material for literature, drama, or the visual arts, where the characteristic nature of individual human beings has again and again been meticulously worked out.

Individual peculiarities are of considerable importance for the social fabric. The differentiated performance society, for example, is a consequence of the view that not all members of a society can accomplish all arising tasks equally well, which is why a targeted placement of persons suggests itself. Qualification characteristics of persons should correspond as well as possible with the requirements of tasks. A constitutive component of the performance society is an educational system that presupposes a differing capacity for learning and trainability among its citizens and that maintains for them numerous educational institutions from special schools to universities, which moreover provide for considerable internal differentiation. Over wide stretches, moreover, the economy lives off the differing demands of people by providing for a great variety of products.

Only at first glance may it seem paradoxical by contrast when, throughout history, the equality of human beings has repeatedly been demanded, as in the Declaration of Independence of the USA (“All men are created equal”), the ideal of the French Revolution (“Liberté, Egalité, Fraternité”), or in trade-union demands (“Equal pay for equal work”). Thereby at least the worst excesses of a politics of servitude and exploitation based on the inequality of human beings were to be opposed, and in part also the asserted (“natural”) necessity of the inequalities themselves was to be contested.

In fact, inequalities exist. Men are still paid better than women despite comparable performance. Older people receive more pay than younger ones. Good-looking persons have an advantage over less attractive ones in the educational process, in psychotherapeutic treatment, or in the choice of a partner.

The series of such examples can be continued at will. What is to be noted is that in many traits interindividual differences exist, some of them, however, of almost no consequence for the individual, and others relevant only in individual situations (e.g., the shape of the inner auditory canals, the propensity to seasickness). Interindividual differences in traits such as sex, skin color, intelligence, or personality are, by contrast, of the greatest importance across situations, because they co-determine the efficacy of human beings upon the world and, conversely, the feedback of the environment upon them.

The description and analysis of such interindividual differences between individuals or groups form the subject matter of Differential Psychology. In contrast to this, General Psychology treats the regularities of the behavior and experience of the average individual. It is not necessarily certain that such an “average” individual exists at all, and that the regularity applies equally well to all members of the population. Put differently, Differential Psychology concerns itself with the systematic portion of the variability in behavior and experience that General Psychology brackets out.

The differential-psychological consideration of differences refers not only to differences between persons at a given point in time (cross-sectional consideration), although this is its main subject matter. The fluctuation of traits within a person across situations or points in time (longitudinal consideration, intraindividual differences) can also be a subject of Differential Psychology. Then interindividual differences in the intraindividual processes are investigated. If such differences do not exist or are of no interest, the processes are the subject of General Psychology or also of Developmental Psychology, depending on whether shorter stretches of time in the range of seconds up to several weeks are involved, or longer periods of time up to many years.

Differential-psychological questions concern:

- the nature of traits or processes in which there are interindividual differences,
- the mutual dependence of such traits,
- the extent of interindividual differences,
- the organismic, cognitive, emotional, and motivational foundations for these differences,
- their causes, including hereditary and environmental factors, as well as
- the prediction of future behavior on the basis of these differences,
- their amenability to influence through training, altered stimulating conditions, medications, and other conditions.

Many of these questions are the subject of this textbook.

### Summary of Section 1.1

Interindividual differences exist in a great many physical and psychological traits; nevertheless, the members of a species are united by a common blueprint. In this field of tension between inequality and equality one can see both something valued in our cultural sphere—such as the particularity and uniqueness of an individual person—and a societal constitution between injustice and justice. Differential Psychology concerns itself, besides interindividual differences, also with intraindividual changes, insofar as they are differently pronounced in different persons.

## Core Field Concepts and Methods

### Core concepts

| Concept (EN) | Concept (DE) | Definition from the text (EN) | Definition from the text (DE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Interindividual differences** | **Interindividuelle Unterschiede / Differenzen** | Differences between individuals (or groups) in physical and psychological traits; some nearly irrelevant, some situation-specific, some important across situations (e.g. sex, skin color, intelligence, personality). | Unterschiede zwischen Individuen (oder Gruppen) in physischen und psychischen Merkmalen; manche nahezu ohne Belang, manche nur in einzelnen Situationen relevant, manche situationsübergreifend von größter Bedeutung (z. B. Geschlecht, Hautfarbe, Intelligenz, Persönlichkeit). |
| **Intraindividual differences / changes** | **Intraindividuelle Unterschiede / Veränderungen** | Fluctuation of traits within one person across situations or time points. | Merkmalsfluktuation innerhalb einer Person über Situationen oder Zeitpunkte hinweg. |
| **Differential Psychology** | **Differentielle Psychologie** | Description and analysis of interindividual differences; also covers intraindividual changes when those differ across persons. It studies the systematic portion of variability that General Psychology brackets out. | Beschreibung und Analyse interindividueller Differenzen; befasst sich auch mit intraindividuellen Veränderungen, sofern sie bei verschiedenen Personen unterschiedlich ausgeprägt sind. Untersucht den systematischen Teil der von der Allgemeinen Psychologie ausgeklammerten Variabilität. |
| **General Psychology** | **Allgemeine Psychologie** | Studies regularities of behavior and experience of the “average” individual (with the caveat that such an average may not really exist). | Behandelt die Gesetzmäßigkeiten des Verhaltens und Erlebens des durchschnittlichen Individuums (wobei unsicher ist, ob es ein solches durchschnittliches Individuum überhaupt gibt). |
| **Developmental Psychology** (boundary) | **Entwicklungspsychologie** (Abgrenzung) | Takes over when the focus is process over longer time spans (up to years), when interindividual differences in those processes are absent or not of interest. | Gegenstand, wenn Prozesse über längere Zeitabschnitte (bis hin zu vielen Jahren) betrachtet werden und interindividuelle Unterschiede in diesen Prozessen nicht bestehen oder nicht interessieren. |
| **Particularity / uniqueness of the person** | **Besonderheit / Unverwechselbarkeit der Person** | Recognizable less in isolated shared traits than in the specific combination of traits, motives, and situational embedding. | Wird weniger in isoliert übereinstimmenden Merkmalen erkennbar als in der spezifischen Kombination der Merkmale, den Beweggründen und der situativen Einbindung. |
| **Common blueprint vs. inequality** | **Gemeinsamer Bauplan vs. Ungleichheit** | Members of a species share a common plan, yet still differ widely—the tension between equality and inequality. | Die Mitglieder einer Spezies eint ein gemeinsamer Bauplan, gleichwohl bestehen weite Unterschiede—das Spannungsfeld zwischen Gleichheit und Ungleichheit. |
